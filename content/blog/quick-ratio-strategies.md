The SaaS Quick Ratio is one of the most critical efficiency metrics in your toolkit. It tells you whether your growth is efficient by comparing the revenue you're gaining to the revenue you're losing. A high Quick Ratio means you're growing without leaking.

## What Is the Quick Ratio?

The Quick Ratio measures growth efficiency by comparing revenue gained (new + expansion) against revenue lost (churn + contraction).

**Quick Ratio = (New MRR + Expansion MRR) / (Churned MRR + Contraction MRR)**

A Quick Ratio of 4.0 means you're gaining $4 for every $1 you lose.

## Benchmarks

| Quick Ratio | Rating |
|-------------|--------|
| 4.0+ | Excellent: Hyper-efficient growth |
| 2.0 - 4.0 | Good: Healthy growth efficiency |
| 1.0 - 2.0 | Average: Needs attention |
| Below 1.0 | Critical: Losing revenue faster than gaining |

## Why It Matters

The Quick Ratio reveals efficiency that other metrics miss. A company growing 20% YoY with a Quick Ratio of 1.5 is spending enormously to overcome churn. A company growing 15% YoY with a Quick Ratio of 4.0 is growing more efficiently and sustainably.

Investors increasingly use the Quick Ratio alongside the Rule of 40 to evaluate SaaS companies. A high Quick Ratio indicates:

- Strong product-market fit (customers stay and expand)
- Efficient go-to-market (minimal wasted spend)
- Sustainable growth trajectory (less capital required)

## How to Improve Your Quick Ratio

### Increase the Numerator

- **Acquire more customers:** Scale what's working in marketing and sales. Track efficiency with our [CAC calculator](/growth-efficiency/cac-calculator)
- **Drive expansion revenue:** Upgrade existing customers, cross-sell, and implement usage-based pricing. Use our [NRR calculator](/revenue/nrr-calculator)

### Decrease the Denominator

- **Reduce churn:** Improve onboarding, build customer health scores, implement proactive retention. Use our [churn calculator](/churn-retention/churn-calculator)
- **Minimize contraction:** Right-size plans before customers downgrade, offer annual discounts, build usage-based components

## Quick Ratio by Stage

| Stage | Typical Quick Ratio |
|-------|-------------------|
| Seed | 3.0 - 5.0 (small base, lots of new) |
| Series A | 2.5 - 4.0 |
| Growth | 2.0 - 3.5 |
| Scale | 1.5 - 3.0 |

As companies mature, the Quick Ratio naturally declines because the denominator grows as the customer base expands. A Quick Ratio above 2.0 at scale is excellent.

## The Interaction With Other Metrics

The Quick Ratio is most effective when analyzed alongside:

- **Net Revenue Retention:** High NRR + High Quick Ratio = exceptional business. Use our [NRR calculator](/revenue/nrr-calculator)
- **Rule of 40:** Use our [Rule of 40 calculator](/growth-efficiency/rule-of-40-calculator)
- **Burn Multiple:** High Quick Ratio + Low Burn Multiple = capital-efficient growth machine

## Common Mistakes

- **Not segmenting:** Quick Ratio varies by customer segment and product line
- **Monthly vs. annual:** Use consistent periods for both numerator and denominator
- **Ignoring contraction:** Churn gets attention, but contraction can be equally damaging

## The Bottom Line

The Quick Ratio is your efficiency scorecard. A high ratio means you're growing efficiently. A low ratio means you're spending too much to overcome churn. Track it monthly and build your operating system around improving it.
