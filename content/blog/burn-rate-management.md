Cash is the lifeblood of any startup, and burn rate is the meter that tells you how fast you're consuming it. Understanding and managing burn rate is arguably the most important financial skill for any SaaS founder.

## What Is Burn Rate?

Burn rate measures how quickly your company spends its cash reserves. There are two types:

- **Gross burn:** Total monthly operating expenses
- **Net burn:** Total monthly expenses minus monthly revenue

Net burn is the more important number because it accounts for the revenue your business generates. A company with $200K in monthly expenses and $150K in MRR has a net burn of $50K/month.

## Calculating Cash Runway

Cash runway tells you how many months until you run out of money:

**Runway = Current Cash Balance / Net Burn Rate**

If you have $2M in the bank and a net burn of $100K/month, your runway is 20 months.

## Healthy Benchmarks

| Stage | Ideal Runway | Notes |
|-------|--------------|-------|
| Seed | 18-24 months | Gives time to find product-market fit |
| Series A | 18-24 months | Should reach $1M+ ARR within runway |
| Growth | 12-18 months | Revenue should cover increasing portion |
| Profitable | N/A | Revenue covers expenses |

## The Burn Multiple

The burn multiple is a more sophisticated measure that compares net burn to net new ARR:

**Burn Multiple = Net Burn / Net New ARR**

A burn multiple below 1.5x is excellent, 1.5-3x is average, and above 3x is concerning. Track yours alongside burn rate for a complete picture.

## How to Extend Runway

### Reduce Costs

- **Audit SaaS subscriptions:** Eliminate unused tools
- **Optimize cloud costs:** Right-size infrastructure, use reserved instances
- **Remote-first:** Reduce office and travel expenses
- **Contractor vs employee:** Use contractors for variable workloads

### Increase Revenue

- **Raise prices:** The fastest way to improve unit economics
- **Focus on highest ROI channels:** Cut low-performing marketing spend
- **Annual contracts:** Improve cash flow with upfront payments
- **Expansion revenue:** Upsell existing customers (lower cost than new acquisition)

## The Danger Zone

Watch for these warning signs:

- Runway below 12 months with no clear path to profitability
- Burn multiple above 3x for multiple quarters
- Revenue growth slowing while burn continues rising
- Multiple rounds of "extend runway" cuts

## Strategic Implications

Your burn rate strategy depends on your stage and goals. If you're pursuing rapid growth and have strong unit economics, a higher burn rate is acceptable. If you're approaching profitability, reducing burn becomes the priority.
